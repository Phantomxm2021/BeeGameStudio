# Comparative feel studies

Use these sources to investigate how an action communicates response, consequence or character. They are examples for judgment, not mandatory techniques, effect counts or an engine choice. Inspect the relevant demonstration before claiming to have compared its motion or sound; a public abstract supports only the stated principle. Prefer examples with a comparable camera, action frequency, material and player fantasy. Record both what transfers and what would look or sound wrong in the current game.

| Primary source | Useful comparison | Limit / counterexample |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Nintendo, *Super Mario Bros. Wonder: Bringing the details to life*](https://www.nintendo.com/au/news-and-articles/ask-the-developer-vol-11-super-mario-bros-wonder-chapter-2/) | The developers show the camera-facing treatment and explain balancing readable travel direction with facial expression; they also describe retiming a pipe-entry sound to match the depicted action. Compare what the player recognizes through the action and cue together. | Inspect the relevant picture or footage; the interview text supports the reported decision, not a measurement of the current game. It prescribes neither Mario's proportions nor extra actions, joints or frames. |
| [Folmer Kelly, *Don't Juice It or Lose It* (GDC public overview)](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1021398/Don-t-Juice-It-or) | Test whether added dust, bounce, gradients or shake belong to the world's material and action, rather than assuming more polish means more feel. | The public overview does not establish that restraint is always superior. A readable arcade action may deliberately exaggerate; a heavy rover on clean metal should not automatically kick up dust. |
| [Apple, *Motion*](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion) | Compare the timing and purpose of repeated feedback: does it acknowledge the input promptly, express the result precisely and avoid making the player wait? Preserve non-motion cues when motion is reduced. | This is platform design guidance, not a fixed timing target or a demand to adopt Apple UI styling in a game. |
| [Godot's implemented player action](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-demo-projects/blob/34fc99545fc41520a958248f607101e7d0b95b05/2d/platformer/player/player.gd) with the [same-version scene study](../game-scene-designer/encounter-design-patterns.md#worked-scene-study-an-existing-platformer) | Trace the source sample's state into facing/pose selection and a legal action into its sound trigger. | Source code establishes wiring, not its audible quality or feel. Reuse the method with the current engine and rules; the demo's abilities and coefficients are not requirements. |
| [Godot, *2D sprite animation*](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/2d/2d_sprite_animation.html) | Separate the asset question (individual views or sheet frames) from playback and state changes. Compare actual directional and stop/start poses rather than treating translation of one image as a complete character animation. | This is a concrete implementation example, not a requirement to use Godot, a sprite sheet or any specified frame count. |

![Godot's official isolated run-animation preview](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/_images/2d_animation_run_preview.gif)

This preview shows changing poses, not a complete gameplay situation or verified state binding. Use it for a pose-continuity question; use the linked playable-scene sources for integration questions. Reading these links does not establish that their pictures, motion or sound were observed.

For the assigned question, retain the observed relationship, source and viewing/listening conditions, what may transfer and what remains uncertain. A still supports composition observations, not timing claims; an isolated audio file does not establish an integrated mix. A source-backed inference can inform a proposed treatment; it is not an observed result in the current game or a license to reuse assets.
