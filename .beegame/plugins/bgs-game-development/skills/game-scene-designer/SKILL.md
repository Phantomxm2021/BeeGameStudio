# Playable Scene and Spatial Design

Design playable scenes from the current brief and established abilities/rules. For a first layout or rebuild, carry forward intended player outcomes and constraints from that brief or adopted design. Existing coordinates and dimensions are candidate implementations, reusable where they serve those decisions under current conditions. Construct the starting view, places to act or decide, needed information and consequence/recovery. Compare spatial arrangements where they change that experience; retain the chosen tradeoff in the existing design/scene source. Scope repairs to the affected relationship.

- Organize activity areas by their information, access and recovery needs. Bring together activities needing a shared view or short transfer; separate those whose traffic or exposure would interfere. Choose boundaries and connections from the intended decisions and actual rule gates; rewards alone do not enforce order. A fixed arena can organize these relationships within one field, without extra rooms or travel.
- Use elevation, depth, relative scale or overlap where they clarify action, orientation or the intended experience. Judge these from expected views and movement: reveal a required destination or cue before the action needs it, and use concealment where intended. Landmarks and changes of enclosure/density should make places distinguishable from actual approaches.
- Choose encounter placement for the audience and intended experience. Only where that experience calls for progression or variation, change information, spatial constraints or pressure for their effect on the player's action. No fixed combat peak or lesson sequence is universal.
- Use established movement, collision and checkpoint rules to size traversable gaps, clearances and recovery space. Where movement or collision rules remain unresolved, retain their dependent dimensions as provisional rather than invent controller coefficients. Express the chosen relationships through a description, map or sketch suited to the question, using the game's grid, free-form or 3D space.

When constructing or changing visual composition or environmental activity, read [Art](../game-art-director-expert/SKILL.md). Scene/spatial design resolves access, scale and visibility/contact for play; Art shapes the subject-specific motion, light and atmosphere. Choose activity and quiet space together so ambient changes enrich the place without masking required cues. Refine placement within the rules and selected design. Carry agreed spatial and independent-control relationships into concepts and authorized runtime scene sources, preserving authored identities, logical geometry, camera and resource bindings. For API mechanics, match the selected engine and version: [Phaser 4](../phaser4-gamedev/SKILL.md) for Phaser 4, [Three.js](../threejs-gamedev/SKILL.md) for Three.js, and corresponding APIs and documentation for Phaser 3 or other engines. Decorative depth remains distinct from collision or traversable space.

Resolve the action's needed visibility and the interface's actual visual/input occupancy, including offsets, in the same host and camera view. For a first composition, start from the actual positions or support surfaces where the relevant action can occur. Use its established reach, contact and recovery ranges at those locations, the subject's visible extent, and the targets or cues needed for that decision to choose framing, scale or interface reflow. When stable framing is intended, retain it where those relationships remain readable; otherwise choose the camera response or composition change for the information that needs to enter or remain in view. Reuse an applicable allocation in relation to those action locations and the current layout. Map authored contact points with the gameplay geometry so visible contact remains aligned. Accommodate minimum display sizes through that composition while keeping the chosen gameplay dimensions authoritative.

Calculate only an unresolved reachability/rule question that changes the spatial choice; retain its assumptions and result. Distinguish geometric distance, traversable distance and playtime assumptions. Observe affected scenes/encounters in the actual game when runtime verification is in scope; label unavailable observations and untested game feel.

For first-time teaching or the development of an encounter/round, as well as a reported misunderstanding or pacing problem, use [player guidance and progression](../../references/game-scene-designer/player-guidance-and-progression.md) to choose what the arrangement reveals, lets the player try and changes next.

For an unresolved arrangement choice, use only the relevant [worked example](../../references/game-scene-designer/encounter-design-patterns.md): the [playable scene](../../references/game-scene-designer/encounter-design-patterns.md#worked-scene-study-an-existing-platformer) traces visibility, contact and scale into scene sources; the [two-room route](../../references/game-scene-designer/encounter-design-patterns.md#worked-route-reasoning-two-rooms-and-a-return) compares topology, information order and return travel. Reuse retained guidance; these are reasoning examples, not level templates.
