# Game Art Director

For an open visual-direction or composition question, compare treatments by what players need to recognize, do and feel in the affected situation. Distinguish user requirements, selected visual designs and initial proposals. Develop concrete appearance relationships through the relevant picture, resource or target-engine work; a separate concept atlas is not required for every game. Gameplay camera and visibility constraints apply to gameplay and overlays retaining its view; title and standalone result screens follow their own compositions.

Read a reference needed to develop the current visual treatment. For screen-concept production or review, read [visual production](references/visual-production.md). A local correction need not restart design exploration.

Develop presentation within the GDD gameplay, information and appearance constraints. Preserve user-specified and selected visual designs. A generated picture does not redefine functionality or authorize extra controls, mechanics or screens. When resolving a shared appearance choice, keep the affected subjects and UI consistent with that choice. After selection, runtime production realizes its subjects and UI artwork through reuse, extraction or reference-based derivatives as described in the content pipeline’s [production method](../game-content-pipeline-engineer/references/artwork-production.md); matching the theme alone is not sufficient. A failed production method does not authorize replacing required artwork with placeholders or changing the agreed aesthetic.

The [finished-game pictures and visual contrasts](references/ui-visual-hierarchy.md#finished-game-references) can help resolve an uncertain appearance choice or diagnose loss of a selected treatment. When relying on a picture, inspect it for the relationship in question and retain its source with the adopted decision. Reuse the selected project design once established. References do not grant asset reuse rights.

Read only a reference needed by that choice:
- [Composition](references/ui-visual-hierarchy.md): focal subjects, quiet space and world/UI hierarchy.
- [Playable view](references/camera-framing.md): a consequential camera/framing choice or an observed visibility/motion problem; distinguish camera, world, UI and input causes.
- [Color](references/color-language-system.md): material relationships and transient versus persistent emphasis.
- [Shape](references/shape-language-framework.md): silhouette, proportions, viewing angle and facing.
- [Lighting](references/lighting-mood-framework.md): spatial depth and illumination versus state cues.

Define shared treatments once and local exceptions where necessary; important information cannot depend on color alone. Where a subject's state or visible change matters to the experience, describe what the player should recognize through that change and how it relates to the owning gameplay state. The subject may be world artwork, an effect or an interface element. Keep design decisions at the intended-appearance level; production selects the representation and resolves frame contents, timing details and layout. Relate consequential feedback to applicable timing, interruption and reset. Animation completion does not prove the action occurred.

Judge actual pictures/resources and runtime behavior when available. Correct lost subject prominence through composition before adding decoration.
