# Game Economy Checks

Use only when the game has earnable or spendable resources. Distinguish a score, collectible, crafting input, and currency before adding sinks or monetization assumptions.

- For each affected resource, identify what the player earns, stores, spends, loses, or carries forward, and why that matters to the intended loop.
- Compare typical acquisition and spending over a representative play window. Include randomness, skill differences, and any known exploit path that changes the outcome.
- Look for unusable surplus, unavoidable scarcity, bypassable progression, and incentives that undermine fun. Accumulation is not automatically a defect; some games intentionally let players stockpile.
- If paid currency exists, treat fairness, transparency, and applicable platform rules as separate product requirements. Do not infer monetization from an ordinary game economy.
- Change the smallest source, cost, limit, or reward relationship that addresses the observed problem, then verify through play or simulation.
