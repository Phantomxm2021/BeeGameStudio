# UI decisions from the player's task

Read for an unresolved information hierarchy, menu-cost or interface tone decision. Keep the answer in the existing UI/art definition; this is not a required study or another specification.

At the relevant moment, what is the player trying to do, what must they perceive, and what is the next available action? Assign each cue a job: current decision, contextual help, urgent exception or emotional punctuation. If a cue has no job here, remove or defer it without losing required information. Count the actual actions to reach and return from a frequent task; omit redundant confirmation and repeated introductions, while preserving consequential commitments and recovery. Test comprehension with appropriate player observation rather than assuming that a readable label was understood.

Choose world-space versus screen-space information from visibility, camera and interaction cost. A label attached to an object can explain a relationship, but occlusion, distance or camera motion may make a stable readout necessary. Check the moving play situation, not only the empty area in a concept. Titles and results can give imagery more room than a combat HUD; use the same material/type/icon family without making their information densities identical. Existing states are scope, not a request to add inventory, onboarding or settings.

## Public examples: choices, not a Nintendo preset

| Source and original context | Useful design question | Counterexample / limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Nintendo's UI/UX role](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/jobs/introduction/design/work03.html): mediating play through pictures/text and improving the experience flow | What does the player see, understand and do across this transition? Does repeated use add avoidable work? | A sequence diagram or repeated polish is not mandatory for a small repair. These principles do not prove comprehension without observation. |
| [Zelda UI designer interview](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/jobs/keyword/150.html): restrained presence with memorable necessary information; loading maps support anticipation of exploration | Which information earns persistent emphasis, and where can the game's subject supply delight? | Hiding necessary combat state harms decisions. A map during unavoidable loading does not justify adding a wait, map or feature. |
| [Switch OS CEDEC summary](https://nintendowire.com/news/2018/08/22/nintendo-talks-about-the-design-of-the-switchs-os-at-cedec-2018/): short responsive transitions and fewer actions | Can acknowledgment happen now while presentation continues? Does a repeated flourish delay return to play? | This is secondary reporting about a system UI, not a universal duration or a reason to remove a necessary confirmation. |

Translate the chosen answer through the existing [motion language](motion-language.md), not through a fixed palette, roundness or bounce. UI motion owns interface acknowledgment/navigation; character animation, VFX, camera and gameplay response retain their existing owners. A celebratory reveal can support a rare achievement while becoming tiring on every retry; quiet feedback can suit exploration while failing to communicate a sudden threat. Judge the relevant normal-speed interaction and its cost, not the animation's complexity.
