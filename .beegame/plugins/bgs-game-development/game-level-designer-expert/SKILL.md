# Game Level Designer

Design spatial flow, tension and learning from the actual abilities and rules. For a spatial or teaching question, compare candidate arrangements by what the player can perceive and do under those rules; record the relationships that resolve the question. Final decorative placement belongs to visual production. Scope repairs to the affected route or encounter; examples add no requirements.

- Connect spatial choices to what players perceive, available actions/routes, pressure, discovery and recovery. Distinguish optional routes from order enforced by rules; reward locations alone do not enforce collection order.
- Make the critical route, affordances and focal points readable through a description, map or sketch of the needed spatial relationships. Use supplied dimensions, or derive them from established movement when they decide the current spatial question. Use landmarks, sightlines, silhouette, density and quiet space deliberately. A small arena needs no extra regions or filler decoration; leave materials and palette to art direction.
- Fit teaching, encounter beats and difficulty to the audience and genre. Setup/escalation/peak/resolution can help an encounter, but neither combat peaks nor fixed early/mid/late lessons are universal. Communicate required actions and hazards sufficiently for the intended fairness.
- Check proposed traversal against established movement, collision, camera and checkpoint rules. Where those inputs do not yet exist, retain the required capability, visibility and recovery relationships as hypotheses for implementation rather than fixing controller coefficients and placements to prove them on paper. Choose grid, free-form or 3D placement for the mechanic; preserve gameplay scale. Static connectivity or valid documents do not prove traversal, runtime safety or pacing.

For visual-only work, carry the spatial relationships and required visibility into the screen concepts. When implementation is authorized, author scenes in the chosen engine’s format using those requirements, selected pictures and actual resources. Preserve authored identities and the relationships among logical sizes, collision geometry, camera and resource bindings in the selected runtime's existing sources. Resource binding must preserve the required logical geometry.

Use actual UI play-area allocation, including host offset, for camera framing. Before allocation exists, state space/visibility needs as provisional. Different screen arrangements must preserve gameplay rules and scale.

Use the selected runtime’s native scene and camera facilities; plain spatial prose does not need implementation details.

Calculate only an unresolved reachability/rule question whose answer changes the spatial choice. Keep its assumptions and result with the decision. Distinguish geometric distance, traversable distance and playtime assumptions. Verify affected traversal/encounters in the actual game when runtime verification is in scope; label unavailable observations and untested game feel.

For a reported misunderstanding or requested progression/round-experience critique, use [player guidance and progression](references/player-guidance-and-progression.md) to choose a behavior hypothesis and relevant observation. No fixed lesson sequence or extra level is implied.

For an unresolved spatial or teaching relationship, read only the relevant [worked example](references/encounter-design-patterns.md): the [existing playable-scene study](references/encounter-design-patterns.md#worked-scene-study-an-existing-platformer) connects a real picture with scene, action and sound sources; the [two-room route](references/encounter-design-patterns.md#worked-route-reasoning-two-rooms-and-a-return) examines topology, information order and return travel. Reuse guidance already in context; these examples are not a required lesson sequence or level template.
