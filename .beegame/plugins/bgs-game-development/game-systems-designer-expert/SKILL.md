# Game Systems Designer

For a gameplay-rule or core-loop question, trace the affected action from its conditions through the resulting state and feedback. Examine what repetition lets the player learn, choose or express when that is the question being resolved. Progression, economy and retention methods apply when the requested game needs those systems.

Record the resolved rule once with its applicable conditions, preserving user requirements and quantities. Resolve ordering or recovery when competing definitions change the player-visible outcome. Controller and cadence coefficients are implementation and playtest choices unless those values are themselves the assigned design question.

For an unfamiliar mechanic or consequential unresolved assumption, reuse current research or consult relevant public sources. Record the conclusion, source and adaptation with the decision. Stop when that question is answered; examples do not override requirements or prove that the proposed game is enjoyable.

Read the reference that answers the open question:
- [Core loops](references/core-loop-contract.md): player actions, consequences and the player promise.
- [Parameter boundaries](references/parameter-boundary-model.md): assigned quantitative analysis, implementation or tuning.
- [State boundaries](references/state-machine-rules-boundary.md): transitions, simultaneous outcomes and recovery.

For an assigned critique or a concrete agency/repetition problem, use [experience questions](references/experience-questions.md) to connect the player promise to a falsifiable hypothesis and scoped revision. This is not a GDD completion checklist. When implementation or tuning is assigned, test the smallest interaction that could contradict the affected rule; document consistency does not establish interesting play.
