# Guidance and progression within the current game

Read for a concrete first-use misunderstanding, route/encounter problem or requested pacing revision. Input: actual scene/state, intended action, target audience's prior knowledge, available inputs and evidence. Output: likely attention/action path, supported confusion or progression risk, a scoped correction and a behavior to observe.

Follow the player's view before following the intended solution: what is visible, what appears actionable, what changes after an attempt, and where failure points next. Compare plausible alternatives, including doing nothing or choosing the wrong object. A tester who knows the solution can test reachability but cannot establish discoverability. Keep that human question open without blocking unrelated technical work.

Prefer relationships that reveal the current mechanic: line of sight, contrast with nearby objects, safe opportunities to try, and clear consequences. Explicit instructions, accessible alternatives and necessary reminders are valid; removing text is not automatically better guidance. UI hierarchy/navigation belongs to the UI specialist; appearance to Art; response to Feel; rules to Systems.

For change over time, inspect what new information, constraint or interaction alters the player's task. Repetition may support mastery or contemplation; identify the actual problem before adding variety. A collection round can vary risk or placement, a platformer landing/obstacle, a puzzle inference, and a strategy game allocation under competing pressure. Sandbox and multiplayer play need not have a prescribed route or ending. Use the game's authored structure, not a universal four-step level or extra modes.

Test the affected starting situation and a relevant later/recovery situation through delivered controls. Record the current assumption and what would disprove the intended learning or challenge. Keep screenshots as evidence of available cues, not evidence that a novice understood them. Where actual player evidence is required, use the existing player-observation reference under its authorization/privacy limits.
