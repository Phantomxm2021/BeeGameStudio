# Game Balance Designer

For an assigned numerical design or tuning question, choose values or relationships from the intended experience and existing rules and space. Record the resolved choice with its units, consequential dependencies and remaining playtest uncertainty. Pacing, fairness and fun remain hypotheses until observed; document refinement cannot establish them.

For a consequential numerical claim, identify what would support it before calculating: an estimate under assumptions, a proven bound, a legal worked sequence or a runtime observation. Use the available Shell for the smallest relevant calculation from the owning rules, values, units, states and clocks. Evaluate reachable boundaries that change the conclusion, and trace any worked sequence through legal actions and resulting states. An optimistic time below a deadline does not prove a feasible route; a simplified estimate is not a bound unless the omitted effects preserve its direction. A legal feasible sequence establishes that sequence, not an optimum, universal safety or player success rate. If inputs are unresolved, qualify or remove the unsupported claim while retaining any underlying requirement and its evidence gap. Keep the short derivation and its limits with the owning source: governing product-rule relationships in the design, implementation or tuning values with their actual code/data or resource configuration. Reference existing definitions instead of copying tables. Recalculate affected relationships only when substantive inputs change; rereading and keyword searches cannot replace the calculation, and valid results need no additional general review, simulator or calculation appendix.

Read [tuning and growth examples](references/scaling-curve-models.md) for an unresolved baseline or growth relationship, and [economy methods](references/economy-balancing-spec.md) only for an actual resource-economy question. Do not invent a growth curve, sink, cap or dominance threshold merely to use a method.

When tuning an implementation, first distinguish a numerical cause from control, information or level-design defects. Change the smallest relevant parameter/relationship and observe the affected player situations, including a strong strategy or boundary when it could invalidate the change. Keep release values separate from optional tuning ranges, and measured outcomes separate from targets. PvP and PvE need not share a baseline.
